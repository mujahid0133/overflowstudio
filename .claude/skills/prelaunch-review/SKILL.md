Run these five passes against the current state of the site, in order. For each, produce a short
report before moving to the next — do not fix anything until all five are done and reviewed.

## 1. Agency detection test
Review the entire website and highlight every sentence, section, or visual treatment that could make
Overflow feel like a generic software agency, outsourcing company, staffing firm, or freelancer
marketplace. Note a rewrite direction for each — sharper and more precise, never more buzzword-heavy.

## 2. Fake company test
Review the website as someone who has never heard of Overflow. Identify every place it could appear
inexperienced, vague, unverified, fabricated, or overly promotional. Identify missing credibility
signals. Do not invent evidence — identify what genuine evidence should be added instead.

## 3. Outcome test
Read the website as a founder, ignoring the methodology. Answer: What business outcome does Overflow
create? What changes for the founder? What changes for the company? What risk is removed? What
happens faster? What burden disappears? If any answer is unclear, name the exact copy or section
causing the problem.

## 4. Strategic control test
Determine whether a skeptical founder could incorrectly believe Overflow wants control of the
company's product strategy. Identify every ambiguous section. The site must make unmistakable that
the client owns vision, strategic decisions, and priorities — Overflow owns execution within agreed
boundaries. Don't make Overflow sound passive; it should sound accountable for execution.

## 5. Final trust test
Act as a founder deciding whether to trust Overflow with an important initiative. List every reason
to trust Overflow and every reason to hesitate, ranked by severity. Do not solve hesitations by
inventing claims — recommend only changes supported by actual information or evidence.

## After all five
Summarize the highest-severity findings across all five tests as a single prioritized list before any
fixes are made.
