# Overflow Studio — Definitive Website Build Specification

> **Precedence.** This document supersedes `docs/overflow-studio-spec.md` wherever the two
> disagree. The older spec was written for an earlier direction and still describes a
> different hero (`KEEP IMPORTANT WORK MOVING WHILE YOUR TEAM TAKES SHAPE`), a different
> navigation (Model / Outcomes / How it works / Case studies / About) and a different CTA
> (`Talk to Overflow`). Where this document is silent, the older spec still applies for
> section-level detail on the non-homepage pages, analytics, SEO and QA.

This is NOT a generic website brief. Do not reinterpret, broaden, simplify, or reposition
the company.

The website must make a visitor understand within seconds:

**Overflow is not a software agency. Not a staff-augmentation company. Not a freelancer
marketplace. Not a consultancy. Overflow is not selling developers.**

**Overflow is an execution company built around Plug-in Departments.**

The first and currently focused department is **Product**. The Product Plug-in Department
gives companies the product execution capacity they need to get important product work
designed, developed and shipped — without first having to build the entire internal
department.

The client retains: product vision, strategy, business decisions, ownership, internal
organizational control.

Overflow takes responsibility for: product execution, product design, development,
coordination, delivery.

The website must communicate this so clearly that a visitor does NOT need to decode the
business model. Do not make the visitor infer what Overflow does. Do not prioritize
cleverness over clarity. Do not write generic agency copy.

**Banned phrases:** "We build amazing digital experiences", "Your trusted technology
partner", "End-to-end solutions", "World-class talent", "Digital transformation",
"Full-service", "Cutting-edge", "AI-powered solutions", "We bring your ideas to life",
"Our services", "Hire developers", "Scale your team", "Extend your team", "Staff
augmentation", "Outsourcing", "Resources", "Manpower", "Rockstars", "Gurus".

Do not turn Overflow into a software agency through visual language either. The entire
website must feel like a NEW ORGANIZATIONAL MODEL, not a prettier development-agency site.

---

## 01 — Core strategic idea

The central problem: **important product work is ready to move, but the internal team does
not yet have the capacity to ship it.**

Companies traditionally respond to a capacity gap by `HIRE → WAIT → ONBOARD → ORGANIZE →
EXECUTE`, or by `OUTSOURCE → HAND OFF → COORDINATE → HOPE`.

Overflow introduces another option: `DEFINE THE OUTCOME → ACTIVATE THE DEPARTMENT →
EXECUTE → SHIP`.

The core concept is **Plug-in Departments**. Think of organizational capacity as modular. A
company should not always have to permanently stack more people, departments, management
layers and overhead onto its organization whenever demand increases. Instead, when a
capacity gap appears, the company can activate the capability required for that period.
When the need disappears, the capacity can disappear too.

## 02 — The modular capacity metaphor

The blocks represent organizational capacity. A company has its existing internal
organization; there is a gap between what it needs to execute and what its current
organization can execute; Overflow is the block that fills the gap.

**Do NOT visually show an endless tower of blocks** — that communicates accumulation. The
metaphor is `ADD CAPACITY → USE CAPACITY → SWAP / REMOVE CAPACITY`, never
`ADD → ADD → ADD → BUILD A GIANT ORGANIZATION`.

```
Internal organization: [ EXISTING CAPACITY ]
Need:                  [        GAP        ]
Overflow:              [  OVERFLOW BLOCK   ]
```

The Overflow block temporarily completes the system. When the requirement changes, the block
can be replaced by another capability. This communicates: *your organization doesn't have to
become permanently bigger just because the work temporarily became bigger.*

Keep the concept abstract and sophisticated. Modular geometric blocks — never literal
branded LEGO bricks, never a toy aesthetic.

## 03 — The website's primary job

Make the right founder think: *"We have an important product initiative. Our team doesn't
currently have enough capacity. We don't want to wait months to hire. We also don't want to
hand the whole thing to an agency. This is exactly what Overflow is for."*

The visitor should understand: what Overflow is; why it exists; what a Plug-in Department
means; why this differs from hiring / agencies / freelancers; what Overflow currently
provides; what happens when they work with Overflow; why they should trust Overflow; what
action to take.

## 04 — Website architecture

No traditional agency structure. **No generic "Services" section.** The website should
function like an argument:

1. Hero
2. The capacity problem
3. The old way
4. The Overflow model
5. Plug-in Departments
6. Product — the first department
7. How it works
8. What stays yours / what we take
9. Proof
10. Why this model
11. Who it is for
12. The future
13. Final CTA

Each section answers the next question in the visitor's mind.

## 05 — Hero

- **Eyebrow:** PLUG-IN DEPARTMENTS FOR IMPORTANT WORK
- **Headline:** YOUR TEAM DOESN'T ALWAYS NEED ANOTHER HIRE.
- **Subhead:** Sometimes it needs the capacity to get important work shipped now.
  Overflow activates the product execution capacity you need — from product design through
  development — without requiring you to build the entire department first.
- **Primary CTA:** ACTIVATE PRODUCT CAPACITY
- **Secondary CTA:** SEE HOW IT WORKS
- **Supporting line:** PRODUCT DESIGN + DEVELOPMENT

The visitor should understand the basic model without scrolling.

## 06 — Hero visual

The hero visual must explain Overflow rather than decorate the page. A modular capacity
system: `[ INTERNAL TEAM ]  [ GAP ]  [ NEXT NEED ]`, where the gap is visibly empty and a
module representing Overflow moves smoothly into it, completing the system.

Sequence: existing blocks stable → a visible gap → the Overflow block approaches → aligns
precisely → locks into place → the system subtly stabilizes → the block can later detach →
another configuration can appear.

Precise and engineered. Not flashy, not gimmicky, not a floating 3D object. No endless
bouncing, no constant rotation, nothing moving simultaneously. Motion must have meaning.

## 07 — Navigation

Logo: OVERFLOW. Items: MODEL, PRODUCT, PROOF, ABOUT. CTA: ACTIVATE CAPACITY.

Never use Services / Solutions / Capabilities / Industries. The navigation itself should
reinforce that Overflow is a model, not an agency catalog.

## 08 — The problem

**Headline:** THE WORK IS READY. YOUR TEAM JUST ISN'T BIG ENOUGH FOR IT YET.

Companies rarely struggle because they don't know what needs to happen. More often, the
problem is capacity. An important product initiative appears. The internal team is already
occupied. Hiring takes time. Founders get pulled into execution. And the work waits.

**Supporting statement:** THE GAP BETWEEN WHAT A COMPANY NEEDS TO DO AND WHAT ITS CURRENT
ORGANIZATION CAN EXECUTE IS AN ORGANIZATIONAL CAPACITY PROBLEM.

Visual: what needs to happen → what the internal team can currently handle → capacity gap.
The gap must be visually obvious; the need expands beyond the capacity boundary and the
system becomes visibly constrained. No sad people, stressed founders, stock photography or
office imagery — this is a systems problem, shown as a systems problem.

## 09 — The old way

**Headline:** THE DEFAULT RESPONSE TO A CAPACITY GAP? BUILD A BIGGER ORGANIZATION.

Timeline one: NEED → RECRUIT → INTERVIEW → HIRE → ONBOARD → ORGANIZE → FINALLY EXECUTE.
Or: BRIEF → HANDOFF → COORDINATION → REWORK → DELIVERY.

Then: *Neither is inherently wrong. They simply aren't always the right answer.*

**Supporting statement:** If the need is temporary, why should the organizational overhead be
permanent?

Nodes appear sequentially and each adds weight; the system becomes slower. Not dramatic, not
negative — the accumulated friction should simply be visually understandable.

## 10 — The new model

**Headline:** MEET THE PLUG-IN DEPARTMENT.
**Subheadline:** A department you can activate when you need the capacity — without building
it from scratch first.

Overflow provides execution departments around defined outcomes. You keep the strategy,
ownership and direction. We provide the specialized capacity required to move the work from
decision to delivery.

Visual: `YOUR COMPANY − CAPACITY GAP + OVERFLOW DEPARTMENT = WORK SHIPPED`. It should behave
like a system diagram, not a marketing gimmick.

## 11 — Modular capacity

**Headline:** DON'T KEEP STACKING. SWAP IN WHAT YOU NEED.

Traditional organizations respond to every new requirement by adding another permanent
layer: another hire, another team, another manager, another process. Over time the
organization becomes heavier than the work requires. Overflow works differently: plug in the
capacity required for the job, use it, ship the work, then keep it, replace it or remove it
as the need changes.

Visual: `[ CORE COMPANY ]` + a gap; insert `[ PRODUCT ]`; the system is complete; remove it;
insert `[ ANOTHER DEPARTMENT ]`. This is an explanation of modular organizational capacity,
not a literal statement about future departments.

Animation rules: slow, physical, precise, magnetic alignment, subtle easing. No bouncing, no
spinning, no unnecessary parallax.

## 12 — What Overflow is

**Headline:** NOT PEOPLE. NOT PROJECTS. A DEPARTMENT.

Overflow is not a pool of developers waiting for tickets, and it isn't an agency waiting for
a specification. We activate a coordinated execution function around a defined outcome —
product thinking, design, engineering, coordination and delivery.

**Large statement:** YOU DON'T BUY THE PEOPLE. YOU ACTIVATE THE CAPACITY.

Show fragmented pieces (Designer, Developer, PM, QA) grouping into PRODUCT DEPARTMENT. The
individual pieces should stop being the focus; the department becomes the primary object.

## 13 — Product, the first department

**Headline:** THE FIRST DEPARTMENT: PRODUCT.
**Subheadline:** Product design + development, activated around an outcome.

Your product doesn't need another disconnected specialist. It needs the capacity to move.
Overflow's Product Department brings the product execution function required to take an
important initiative from direction through design, development and shipment.

Progression: DIRECTION → PRODUCT → DESIGN → BUILD → TEST → SHIP. **Do NOT label these as
"services"** — they are stages of execution. As each stage activates, the previous stage
remains connected; the system should feel continuous.

## 14 — Product capabilities

No conventional service grid. Instead: THE PRODUCT DEPARTMENT CAN ASSEMBLE WHAT THE OUTCOME
REQUIRES — PRODUCT, DESIGN, ENGINEERING, AI, RESEARCH, INFRASTRUCTURE, QA, ANALYTICS.

AI is not an independent offer. It appears only as a capability that can be activated when
the product outcome requires it — the same for infrastructure, analytics and research.

**THE OUTCOME DETERMINES THE TEAM.** Not: the team determines what we sell.

## 15 — Ownership

**Headline:** YOU KEEP THE COMPANY. WE TAKE THE EXECUTION.

| Yours | Overflow |
| --- | --- |
| Product vision | Product execution |
| Strategy | Design |
| Business decisions | Development |
| Priorities | Coordination |
| IP | Delivery |
| Ownership | Technical implementation |
| Final authority | Day-to-day execution decisions |

**Supporting line:** YOU DON'T HAND OVER THE PRODUCT. YOU GIVE THE WORK THE CAPACITY IT NEEDS.

The two columns must remain visually distinct with a central boundary. **The client's side
must never visually collapse into Overflow's side.** This communicates partnership and
execution integration, NOT acquisition or outsourcing.

## 16 — How it works

**Headline:** FROM CAPACITY GAP TO SHIPPED PRODUCT.

1. **DEFINE THE OUTCOME** — We establish what needs to exist, why it matters and what
   "shipped" means.
2. **ACTIVATE THE DEPARTMENT** — We assemble the product execution capacity the outcome
   actually requires.
3. **EXECUTE** — Product, design and engineering move together instead of waiting on
   separate functions.
4. **SHIP** — The work is tested, delivered and moved into the client's organization.
5. **CONTINUE, TRANSFER OR RELEASE** — The department stays involved, hands over, or steps
   away depending on what the company needs next.

Use a progress line that fills as the user scrolls. No aggressive scroll-jacking, no
horizontal-scroll trap. The animation should enhance normal scrolling.

## 17 — Why not hiring

**Headline:** HIRING ISN'T WRONG. WAITING IS SOMETIMES THE PROBLEM.

Permanent teams are valuable, but permanent capacity isn't always the immediate requirement.
Sometimes the company needs execution now while its long-term organization is still taking
shape. Overflow exists for that gap.

`BUILD INTERNALLY: Recruit · Interview · Hire · Onboard · Organize · Execute`
`ACTIVATE OVERFLOW: Define · Activate · Execute · Ship`

**Do not attack hiring.** The message is: HIRING SHOULD NOT DETERMINE WHEN IMPORTANT WORK CAN
BEGIN.

## 18 — Why not an agency

**Headline:** AND NO — WE'RE NOT AN AGENCY WITH A NEW NAME.

An agency typically starts with *"What would you like us to build?"* Overflow starts with
*"What important outcome needs to happen — and what execution capacity is missing?"*

`Agency model: PROJECT → HANDOFF → DELIVERY`
`Overflow model: OUTCOME → DEPARTMENT → EXECUTION → SHIPMENT`

Do not insult agencies. Do not claim every agency is bad. Simply explain that Overflow is
designed around a different operating model.

## 19 — Proof

**Headline:** THE MODEL ONLY MATTERS IF IT MOVES THE WORK.

- **8 WEEKS → 3 WEEKS** — Planned work compressed from eight weeks to three.
- **8–10 HOURS** — Founder time recovered every week.

**Disclaimer (required):** These are early proof points, not promises.

Do not fabricate additional metrics, clients, logos or testimonials. If testimonial wording
is unavailable, do not create one. If a case study lacks verified details, present only the
verified facts. The metrics should feel factual and weighty — never bounce like a SaaS
landing page.

## 20 — Who it is for

**Headline:** FOR COMPANIES WITH SOMETHING IMPORTANT TO SHIP.

Overflow is built for companies that already know what they need to build; have an internal
team; have an important initiative ahead; don't currently have enough execution capacity;
don't want hiring to delay the work; want to keep strategic control; want the work actually
shipped.

**Not a fit if:** you only want cheap developers; you need unlimited scope; you don't have a
defined outcome; you want to outsource strategic ownership; you are simply looking for
additional headcount.

This section should qualify leads without sounding exclusionary or arrogant.

## 21 — Customer mental state

**Headline:** THE BEST OUTCOME? "IT'S OFF MY PLATE."

`BEFORE: important initiative + limited capacity + hiring delay + founder involvement`
`AFTER: clear outcome + activated capacity + execution moving + founder bandwidth returned`

The emotional territory is relief, confidence, momentum. The before state should feel
slightly constrained; the after state open, stable and moving. No cartoonish emotional
animation.

## 22 — Operating philosophy

**Headline:** CAPACITY SHOULD BE AS FLEXIBLE AS THE WORK.

Companies change. Priorities change. Demand changes. The capabilities required today may not
be the capabilities required next year. The organization should not have to become
permanently heavier every time the work changes.

Activate what you need. Own what matters. Ship what matters. Change when the work changes.

## 23 — Future vision

**Headline:** PRODUCT IS ONLY THE BEGINNING.

Today, Overflow is focused on one thing: product execution. Over time, the same Plug-in
Department model can extend into other specialized functions — PRODUCT, AI, DATA,
INFRASTRUCTURE, SECURITY and other specialized execution functions.

**Do not present these as current services. Do not make promises about launch dates.** One
department. Master it. Prove it. Expand. The Product block remains the strongest and most
developed; potential modules stay visually secondary.

## 24 — The big idea

**YOU SHOULDN'T HAVE TO BUILD THE ENTIRE DEPARTMENT BEFORE YOU CAN GET THE WORK DONE.**

**THAT'S WHAT OVERFLOW IS FOR.**

Minimal. Huge typography. Lots of whitespace. No unnecessary UI. This should feel like the
conclusion of the entire argument.

## 25 — Final CTA

**Headline:** WHAT NEEDS TO SHIP?
**Subheadline:** Tell us the outcome your team needs to move. We'll determine what execution
capacity it requires.
**CTA:** ACTIVATE PRODUCT CAPACITY · **Secondary:** TALK TO OVERFLOW
**Small line:** PRODUCT DESIGN + DEVELOPMENT

Never "Book a discovery call". Never "Let's build something amazing". No generic contact
language.

## 26 — Footer

OVERFLOW STUDIO — Plug-in Departments for important work. Links: PRODUCT, MODEL, PROOF,
ABOUT. CTA: ACTIVATE CAPACITY.

**Footer statement:** Important work shouldn't have to wait for organizational capacity to
catch up.

## 27 — Visual identity

Must not resemble: generic SaaS, software agency, AI startup, Web3 company, developer
portfolio, dark-mode tech template.

Avoid: generic gradients, purple-blue AI gradients, random 3D blobs, floating dashboards,
stock photos, code screenshots, glassmorphism, neon cyberpunk, excessive rounded cards,
generic startup illustrations.

The visual world should feel like PREMIUM EXECUTION INFRASTRUCTURE: precise, modular,
kinetic, sophisticated, minimal, human, technical, confident, architectural, modern,
editorial, systematic. Expensive because of restraint, not decoration.

## 28 — Color palette

| Role | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Primary background | `#0B0B0B` near-black / deep charcoal |
| Primary text | `#F4F1EA` warm off-white |
| Secondary text | `#A7A39B` muted warm gray |
| Primary accent | `#FF6A00` strong orange |
| Structural neutral | `#242424` |
| Optional highlight | `#FFF3DC` warm cream |

Orange must be used intentionally: active capacity, important actions, system transitions,
key emphasis, CTA, progress, selected states. **Do NOT flood the website with orange.** The
mental association should become: ORANGE = OVERFLOW CAPACITY IS ACTIVE.

## 29 — Typography

Editorial and technical. A highly legible modern grotesk — Inter, Suisse International, Neue
Montreal, Geist or Manrope. Headlines large, heavy, tight tracking, tight line-height. Body
clean, readable, moderate line length.

Avoid futuristic display fonts, novelty geometrics, developer-code aesthetics and luxury
serifs. The brand should feel intelligent, not fashionable for its own sake.

## 30 — Layout system

Strong grid. Large margins. Large typography. Controlled whitespace. Asymmetrical
compositions where appropriate. Do not make every section a centered card. Use full-width
statements, split layouts, system diagrams, modular blocks, large numerical proof, editorial
composition. Whitespace is part of the premium identity.

## 31 — Animation principles

Animation is not decoration. Every animation must communicate capacity, flow, connection,
activation, progress, transformation, modularity or momentum. If it doesn't, remove it.

Use magnetic alignment, smooth translation, subtle scale, opacity, progressive reveals,
structural transitions, counter animations, line drawing, block insertion/removal.

Avoid constant floating, random rotation, excessive parallax, bouncy easing, particle
explosions, glitch effects, cursor gimmicks, scroll-jacking, long loading sequences, and
anything that slows comprehension.

## 32 — Scroll behavior

Normal vertical scrolling. Animations trigger naturally as sections enter the viewport. Use
scroll progress only where it genuinely helps explain the system. **Do NOT make users scroll
through an animation before they can continue.** The user should feel "I am moving through
the story", not "the website is controlling me".

## 33 — Microinteractions

Buttons: subtle movement, orange activation state, an arrow that moves slightly on hover. No
huge hover animations. Links get subtle underline/position transitions. Cards must not tilt.
The website should feel engineered.

## 34 — Cursor

A custom cursor may be used ONLY if it improves the experience. **Do not use a giant circle
following the mouse.** No gimmicky cursor effects. Accessibility and usability come first.

## 35 — 3D

Optional. Do not add 3D because modern websites use it. If used it must support the modular
capacity system and feel architectural and premium — no literal LEGO branding, no toy
aesthetic, no unnecessary reflections, no giant spinning objects.

## 36 — Imagery

Photography is secondary. If used, real human/product/work imagery only. Avoid stock startup
teams, handshakes, laptop-staring and generic offices. The primary storytelling mechanism is
systems and typography.

## 37 — Intended mental transformation

- Before visiting: *"Overflow = probably another software agency."*
- After hero: *"Overflow provides product execution capacity."*
- After Plug-in Department: *"They activate a department rather than sell me developers."*
- After ownership: *"I keep control. They handle execution."*
- After product: *"They do this specifically for product design + development."*
- After proof: *"They've demonstrated speed and founder time recovery."*
- After the final section: *"This is an alternative to waiting for hiring when we have
  important work to ship."*

## 38 — Positioning hierarchy

1. Overflow is an execution company.
2. The model is Plug-in Departments.
3. The first department is Product.
4. Product = design + development + required execution capacity.
5. The capacity is activated around a defined outcome.
6. The client retains strategic control.
7. Overflow owns execution and delivery.

Never reverse this. Do not make "software development", "AI" or "design" the headline. Do not
make individual skills the headline.

## 39 — Verbal identity

Confident, intelligent, direct, sharp, calm, conversational, minimal, slightly provocative,
technically credible. Write like a founder/operator who has already solved the problem.
Short sentences. Strong statements. Concrete language.

Prefer "Get the work shipped" over "Unlock transformative digital potential"; "Activate the
capacity" over "Access world-class talent"; "Keep the strategy. We take the execution." over
"We partner with you to bring your vision to life."

## 40 — Words to emphasize

Capacity · Execution · Outcome · Department · Product · Design · Development · Ship ·
Ownership · Momentum · Activate · Internal team · Important work · Direction · Delivery ·
Capability · Precision

## 41 — Words to avoid

Never as primary positioning: agency, outsourcing, staff augmentation, resources, manpower,
talent pool, freelancers, developers for hire, body shop, full-service, digital
transformation, world-class, rockstars, gurus, cutting-edge, trusted technology partner,
end-to-end solutions, AI-powered, scale your team.

## 42 — Brand philosophy

Hiring is not the only way to increase organizational capacity. A company can own its
strategy and permanent organization while flexibly activating execution capacity around
temporary or changing needs. Overflow does not replace internal teams — it fills the gap
between what the company needs to do and what the current organization can execute.

## 43 — Claim discipline

Never invent clients, case studies, metrics, testimonials, revenue, team size, credentials,
partnerships, awards or results.

Known proof, and the only proof: **8 weeks → 3 weeks**, and **8–10 founder hours recovered
per week**. Use these as factual statements about work already done, never as guaranteed
future outcomes. Do not say "We'll cut your timeline by 60%". Say "One engagement compressed
eight weeks of planned work into three."

## 44 — Responsive design

Desktop: large visual compositions. Mobile: do NOT simply shrink the desktop. The core mental
model must survive mobile — the hero must still clearly communicate the headline and subhead,
the modular block animation should simplify, and there must be no horizontal overflow, no
tiny system diagrams and no unreadable typography. Buttons must be thumb-friendly.

## 45 — Accessibility

Mandatory. High contrast, readable typography, keyboard navigation, reduced-motion support.
Under `prefers-reduced-motion`, disable large transitions and replace animations with static
diagrams. **Never make understanding dependent on animation.**

## 46 — Performance

The site should feel fast. Do not sacrifice performance for animation. Lazy-load heavy
assets, avoid large 3D models, keep animation GPU-efficient, avoid dozens of simultaneous
animated elements. The site should itself demonstrate the quality of execution it sells.

## 47 — How the website should feel

Like entering a highly sophisticated operating system — not a software agency, startup
template, consultancy or creative portfolio. It should communicate: there is a gap; Overflow
fills it; the work moves; the capacity can change; the company remains in control.

## 48 — The single most important visual metaphor

```
[ COMPANY ] → [ CAPACITY GAP ] → [ OVERFLOW ] → [ COMPLETE SYSTEM ]
```

This should recur in different visual forms throughout the site: the hero (block fills gap),
the problem (gap appears), the Plug-in Department (department fills gap), Product (product
department activates), ownership (client + Overflow as connected systems), the future (new
departments swap in), and the finale (work moves because the gap is gone).

## 49 — Final brand statement

**Overflow gives companies the execution capacity they need without requiring them to build
the entire department first.**

## 50 — Final quality test

Can someone understand what Overflow is in 5 seconds? Explain the Plug-in Department model
after 30 seconds? Explain why it differs from an agency? From additional headcount?
Understand that Product is the current focus, and that it means integrated design +
development? That the client retains strategic ownership and Overflow owns execution?
Understand the capacity-gap problem and the modular block concept? See why hiring isn't
always the immediate answer? See real proof? Identify who it is and isn't for? Find the CTA?
Remember "Overflow = Plug-in Departments"?

If any answer is no, the website is not finished.

## 51 — Absolute final rule

**DO NOT MAKE OVERFLOW LOOK LIKE A SOFTWARE AGENCY** — even if the site looks beautiful, the
animations are impressive and the code is excellent. If the visitor leaves thinking "cool
software development agency", the website has failed.

The successful reaction is: *"They're not selling developers. They're selling execution
capacity as a department. They plug the missing capacity into the company, get the important
work shipped, and the company doesn't have to build the whole department first."*
